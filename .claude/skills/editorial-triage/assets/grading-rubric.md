# Grading rubric for editorial triage

The rubric is the shared definition of how a review finding is closed. Every finding gets exactly one closing move; the `GRADER` worker predicts the move, `grade-triage.sh` measures how often the prediction matches the user's move, and the partition rules implement the rubric. The frame is closing, not opening: a finding is resolved by a small change to the text or by recording an open point with an owner, never by a question that invites more text. Change the rubric before changing a partition rule.

A heuristic states a rule of editing and the categories the rule governs. It carries no record of the run, the round or the person that produced the rule, and nothing here points at one: a rule is applied on its own terms. A rule that holds only for one document set is a run note for that review, never a heuristic here.

## The closing moves

| Key | Move | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `R` | Remove | Delete a whole unit: a sentence, a bullet, a table row, a paragraph. The passage's point stands without the removed unit. `R` never cuts a fragment out of a sentence that survives. |
| `C` | Correct | Apply the specific correction the set or a published source establishes: a citation section, a count, a name, a definite article that asserts an undecided choice, the side of a contradiction that has a home. Also the move when the defect sits inside a sentence that must survive and the replacement states a fact the set settles. |
| `W` | Reword | Restate the sentence under a named editorial rule so that the defective part is gone, keeping every claim of the unit; any growth is one of the review brief's two cases, with `loss` saying what the addition makes clear. This is the move whenever the defect sits inside a sentence the passage needs. |
| `K` | Keep one side | A contradiction the set does not settle: one statement survives, the other goes. The user picks; the tool proposes a default. |
| `O` | Record as open | The passage's point depends on a fact the set does not hold. The point is recorded in one line with an owner, for the user to take to that owner; the passage is left as a plain statement of what is undecided. Nothing is asked in chat. |
| `I` | Ignore for now | Nothing changes. The default for style, for omissions whose rule has a home elsewhere, and for cards closed by another card. |
| `T` | Tell me what to do | The user states the closing instruction in their words. The grader never predicts `T`; an instruction that generalises becomes a heuristic. |

The score is the share of grades predicting `R`, `C` or `W` among graded clusters: a process measure of what the estate closes without the user, which consults neither the plan's eligibility nor applied edits and says nothing about the writing. A low closure share is not evidence of poor prose or of a poor review; agreement with the user's labels is a separate measure. An addition carries the burden of proof: a move that adds words is allowed only in the two cases the review brief names, with `loss` saying what the addition makes clear (`review-brief.md`, "Rules for every reviewer").

## Heuristics

Each entry: id, statement, and the categories the heuristic governs.

- **H-01 Judgements are removed, not qualified.** A judgement of ease, cost, safety or simplicity standing in for a fact ("a rotation is cheap and signs nobody out", "easy", "simple", "safe") is cut. The fact, if one is needed, is stated without the judgement. Governs every category.
- **H-02 Undecided mechanisms are not described.** Where the design has not decided a mechanism (the delegation grant source, the key rotation procedure, provider selection), the text does not describe one. Cut the detail and state only that the design must handle the consideration. Governs propose/grows, author, fix/verified on contradiction.
- **H-03 A missing explanation is first a suspect claim.** When a finding says a passage fails to explain or name something, ask whether the passage should make the claim at all. A second justification for a decision the paragraph already justifies is removed; a reassurance that a later caveat withdraws is removed. Governs propose/grows, propose/dispersion, propose/tangent.
- **H-04 Pointers are removed unless the reader cannot find the target without them.** Cross-page and same-page pointers atrophy: their targets move, get renamed or vanish, and every one is a dependency to maintain. A pointer whose only job is to announce where something is listed ("listed under Open questions below") goes; a pointer that loops or names a target that does not exist goes. A pointer is kept and fixed (`W` to a real heading) only when the reader would otherwise be unable to find the reason behind a rule ("see the session lifetimes below"). A one-line fact may be restated in place of a pointer. Governs fix/verified, propose/removal, propose/restructure, propose/tangent.
- **H-05 Rules are not restated at their instances.** A rule with a home elsewhere in the set is not repeated where an instance occurs, and the omission is not a defect. Governs discard/tangent, propose/grows, author.
- **H-06 A claim the set contradicts is cut, not hedged.** When another statement in the set says a stated fact is unverified, drop the clause rather than qualify the clause. Governs propose/removal, fix/verified on contradiction.
- **H-07 An undecided choice is not asserted.** Wording that asserts a decided option ("the corporate Entra tenant") is corrected to the indefinite form in one word; no growth. Governs propose/inference, propose/grows.
- **H-08 A design or ownership question is not an editorial finding.** Who decides a design point, what the design should be, and which team answers a question are outside what editorial review owns, which is the text's coherence with itself. Where the text alludes to such an undecided point and the allusion is the defect, remove the allusion (`R`); otherwise ignore the finding (`I`). `O` records a point only where the user asks for one, and then as a single line with an owner, never as a question in chat and never as new text in the page. Governs author, propose/restructure.
- **H-09 One move can close other cards.** A move on one card can resolve other findings on the same passage; the tool records which cards are closed by which, and the closed cards take `I`.
- **H-10 Verified corrections are applied.** A wrong citation section, count, name or actor corrected against a named published source or against the page that holds the fact takes `C`, provided the span occurs once in the page and the rewrite passes the gate. Governs fix/verified.
- **H-11 Rule-derived rewrites apply only when the record carries them and any growth is one the brief allows.** A rewrite whose basis is a named `language.md` or `document-types.md` section takes `W` when the section covers the text, the rewrite keeps every claim of the unit, and any growth is one of the review brief's two cases with `loss` saying what the addition makes clear; a rewrite that grows outside those cases takes `I`, or `R` under H-03. The grader grades the rewrite the record carries; it never composes a new one, so an item with no rewrite on the record cannot take `W` or `C`. Governs fix/verified on compression, flow, term, residue; propose/preference.
- **H-12 A contradiction closes as a choice of side only when both sides are whole statements.** When each side is a statement claiming the design, the move is `K`: one survives and the other goes, with the side that has the home as the default. The home is the structural home of the fact, a mechanism against a bare assertion, or the majority of three or more statements; a preview, summary or restatement of the design is the side that goes. When the contested text is instead a clause inside a sentence that stands without the clause, and the other side is not itself in question, the contradiction is not a choice: the clause goes under `R` if its unit can go, else the sentence is reworded without the clause under H-19. Putting such an item to the user as a choice of side asks them to pick between a sentence and a fragment. Governs every category on kind contradiction.
- **H-13 A guess is never applied.** A proposal that supplies a mechanism, reason or fact the pages do not state, even by one inference, never takes `C` or `W`; under H-02 the guessed detail is cut. An inference with one cited step from the set is not a guess (H-15). Governs propose/inference, author.
- **H-14 Correctness against the world is out of scope.** Editorial review and its triage own the text's coherence with itself, not its accuracy against sources outside the set: an RFC section number, a vendor's documented behaviour, a platform fact. Such claims are fact-checked by a separate process. A finding that rests on external correctness is not corrected: detail the passage does not need is removed (`R`); otherwise the finding is ignored (`I`). Only a contradiction between the set's own statements counts as a contradiction. Governs fix/verified on fact and citation, propose/removal, author.
- **H-15 An incomplete promise is completed.** When a passage states its own purpose (what a prefix guarantees; the cases a walkthrough step covers; the environments a defence covers) and its text does not meet that purpose, and the set supplies the missing part (a stated condition, the other case, a fact the same page states elsewhere), the completion takes `C` and the growth is allowed. The test is the passage's own promise, not a reader's curiosity; H-03 still governs additions the passage never promised. A defence or rule stated for one environment when the page itself places the service in another is an incomplete promise. Governs propose/grows, propose/inference, propose/omission.
- **H-16 Review residue is removed.** A finding that is the review's own meta-line (a legend, a gate statement, a note about the report) takes `R`: the finding is dropped, not ignored, so it never reappears. Governs propose/unresolved, discard.
- **H-17 Equivalent moves count as agreement.** When a correction's text equals the removal's result, the user and the grader choosing differently between the two counts as agreement. The card still offers both, because after H-19 they are different edits: the removal deletes a whole unit, the correction restates a sentence that survives.
- **H-18 The document's own tallies are removed, not corrected.** A count the passage offers about its own bookkeeping (how many registration families, how many rows, how many secret updates, how many decisions) is cut rather than fixed: the reader needs the things, not the number of them, and the number is what goes wrong when the things change. Governs fix/verified on fact, propose/grows, author.
- **H-19 Remove the whole unit, or reword the sentence; never cut a fragment out of a surviving sentence.** A defect inside a sentence the passage needs is closed by rewording that sentence so the defective part is gone, not by deleting the span and leaving whatever remains. `R` applies when the whole sentence, bullet, row or paragraph goes, and also when the span is a complete parenthetical aside, because deleting one leaves the sentence around the aside whole. A pointer in brackets, such as `(see Cookie design, in Cookies and token transport)`, is the common case. Cutting a span mid-sentence produces a bare noun, a stranded subject, a lowercase opening or a line that fails the style gate, and the reviewer's own rewrite is usually already on the record. So ask first whether the whole unit can go, and reword when it cannot. Governs every category.
- **H-20 Retired.** The numbering skips this id and no later rule reuses the number. The rule that held the id applied to one document set rather than to editing technical documentation, so the rule left the rubric. Grades from earlier runs still cite the id, and the tooling reports such a citation as suspect.
- **H-21 A sentence that narrates the document rather than the design is redundant.** Text reporting what the document does, does not do, or has already settled ("that detail is not repeated here", "the IDM facts are established, but the migration approach needs product decisions", "as context for that follow-up") tells the reader what the document itself already shows. The whole sentence goes under `R`, or the containing sentence is reworded without the clause. This is a repetition defect, not an omission. Governs propose/tangent, propose/removal, fix/verified on compression and residue.
- **H-22 A claim about what the document provides is corrected when more is known.** Where the text states what data, list or section the document offers, and the set holds material the statement does not account for, the move is `C` on the statement. The claim is wrong, so correct the claim; do not add material to satisfy the claim, and do not ask. This is an omission defect and is distinct from H-21. Governs author, propose/grows, propose/restructure.

- **H-23 Shorten only when the unit has to survive.** A passage that runs long is closed by removing whole units, never by rewriting the passage shorter. Take `R` on each unit whose removal leaves the point standing, and reach for `W` only where the unit has to stay and the defect sits inside a sentence that survives (H-19). Compression to save words, a restatement of surviving material in words the page never had with no reader difficulty named, is a guess (H-13) and takes `I`. A claim-preserving clarity repair is a different thing: a reword that keeps every claim of the unit and closes a named reader difficulty, and it may be shorter or longer than the source. Under `clarify` such a repair takes `W` when the record's rule names the difficulty and the candidate is eligible under H-29, and any growth is one of the review brief's two cases with `loss` saying what the addition makes clear, which partition rule 6 admits up to `growSeverityMax`. Word-count reduction is neither proof of a guess nor proof of a repair. The choice between removing and rewording is settled by whether the unit can go, never by how long the passage is. Governs propose/removal, propose/tangent, fix/verified on compression and flow.
- **H-24 A flow finding removes the sentence a restatement dropped.** A `flow` finding from the paragraph restatement test in `language.md`, "Paragraph Restatement Test", names one sentence whose removal leaves the paragraph's point standing, and the move is `R` on that sentence. Check the claim rather than taking the finding's word: read the paragraph, restate the paragraph, and take `I` when the restatement keeps that sentence after all. Several `flow` findings on one paragraph are independent, because each names a different sentence, so closing one does not close the others under H-09. Governs propose/removal on kind flow.
- **H-25 A paragraph holding more than one topic closes by a split at the seam.** Under `clarify`, a `split` record takes `W` when the grader confirms the reader difficulty (the paragraph holds more than one topic and the reader meets the second before the first closes), the break falls at the seam (`language.md`, "Sentences and paragraphs", the seam rule), and the candidate is eligible under H-29 (for a split: `layoutOnly` with the changed page passing the gate, or the Q11 conditions). Carrying `action: split` or sitting in `fix/layout` is not sufficient; a split that leaves the prose as hard to read takes `I`, and a long paragraph on one topic is not split. Governs fix/layout, propose/fallback on kind flow.
- **H-26 Discrete facts whose order carries no meaning close by a list of the same words.** Under `clarify`, a `list` record takes `W` when the grader confirms the benefit: criteria, options or facts whose order carries no meaning become bullets, or an ordered mechanism whose every step names its actor becomes a numbered list, with the same words in the same order and the candidate eligible under H-29. A connected argument, a causal explanation or a qualification stays prose, so an unsuitable list proposal takes `I`. Governs fix/layout, propose/fallback on kinds flow and compression.
- **H-27 A sentence whose framing obscures the behaviour or introduces an unsupported judgement is restated as its supported consequence, or removed.** A sentence that frames ("the second difference is the one a user will notice"), pivots figuratively ("that silence has an edge") or ranks without grounding ("a nuisance", "more serious") takes `W` to the consequence the passage supports (the picker appears; credentials are asked for again) or `R` when no supported consequence remains; never a qualification of the judgement, and never a promotion of an ungrounded claim into an established effect. A sentence that states the passage's scope or carries the reader between points is not a defect for framing material (`language.md`, "Voice and tone", "Register"). H-01 removes the bare judgement; H-27 covers the sentence built around one. Governs fix/verified and propose/fallback on kinds fact, compression, flow; propose/preference.
- **H-28 An unclear reference closes by the name the source establishes.** "It", "this" or "that design" that the reader cannot resolve at once and without error takes `W` to the name when the source settles which thing is meant; when the source does not settle the antecedent, the item is a question for the author (`O` on an `author` record, else `I` with the question in `why`), never a guessed name. A pronoun whose referent is the one thing the previous clause named takes `I` (`language.md`, "References and pronouns"). Governs fix/verified on kinds ambiguity, compression, term; propose/preference.
- **H-29 A rewrite or removal is graded as applicable only on its own candidate's verdict.** The rule is the contract's (`assets/editorial-triage-schema.md`, Closing files); the grader applies it before returning any `R`, `C` or `W`, wherever in the order of work the diagnosis was made. The grader reads the cluster's edit record: the lead for `R`; for `C` or `W` the lead when it carries a rewrite, else the first member that does. The removal candidate is the record's real `removes` span when it names one, else its deletion (`before` with an empty `after`); there is no candidate otherwise. Its verdict is the record's own `removalShift`, except that a deletion whose `before` equals its `removes`, and a deletion with no real `removes`, take the rewrite's `claimShift` as the removal's verdict. `none` permits `R`; `material` permits `R` only with the verifier's non-empty note, and the information the removal takes away is then shown in the spot check (Q10); `missing`, `stale` or `unresolved`, no candidate, or a span other than the candidate hold, so the grade is `I` with the state named. A grader's own account of what the removal takes away cannot replace the verifier's note, and a `material` verdict on a different rewrite candidate gives no permission to remove a unit. `C` and `W` are graded only when the rewrite record's `claimShift` is `none` (which the nominator finding nothing also gives), its `introducedCheck` is not `held`, and a replacement spanning lines meets Q11 (`layoutOnly` with `gate` pass, or an explicit unnominated candidate with `gate` pass, or a nominated candidate verified `none`). A held introduction on a rewrite does not bar an independently permitted removal, and a missing rewrite verdict does not bar a verified removal. `missing` and `stale` are states of the run: the grade names them and takes `I`; `material` or `unresolved` on a rewrite takes `R` of the unit under H-03 or H-06 when the removal candidate's own verdict permits, else `I` with the verifier's note quoted. Governs propose/unverified, propose/stale, propose/claim-shift, propose/introduced, and every editing grade in any category.
- **H-30 A presentation finding closes as an open item with its inventory.** A cluster whose kinds hold `presentation`, or any of whose members is a presentation record, takes `O` before any other step of the order of work, owner as the record names, and the inventory goes with the item to the diagramming skill; the grader never draws, never proposes an arrow the text does not state, and never grades the cluster `R`, `C`, `W` or `K`. Governs author/presentation.
- **H-31 A condition the reader meets after the action or information it governs closes by a reorder that keeps every word and the condition's scope.** Under `clarify`, a `rewrite` record that moves a condition before what it governs, keeping every word and the distinctions `if`, `only if`, `unless` and `after`, takes `W` when the reader would otherwise miss the applicability or have to re-read to find what the condition binds (`language.md`, "Sentences and paragraphs", the condition-first rule) and the candidate is eligible under H-29; a contrast or an emphasis that already works takes `I`; when the source does not settle which action the condition governs, the item is a question for the author (`O` on an `author` record, else `I` with the question in `why`), never a guessed scope. Keeping every word is this rubric's restriction on the candidate, narrower than the evidence behind the rule. Governs fix/verified and propose/fallback on kinds compression and ambiguity.
- **H-32 FAQ voice closes by reword, never by ignore.** A heading, table header, caption, bold lead-in or introducing list item in FAQ voice, or a sentence or table cell that opens on a half-question (`language.md`, "Headings, lists and tables"), takes `W` to the noun phrase in title case for a label, or to the actor-first sentence for prose, when the record carries the candidate and the candidate is eligible under H-29. When no record carries a candidate, the grade is `T` with the instruction "name the topic as a noun phrase" (or "put the actor first"), never `I`: the smell does not stay on the page by default, and the user's answer is the label. A sentence that opens on When or Where and states a condition is not FAQ voice and takes `I`. Governs fix/verified and propose/fallback on kinds term, compression and flow; propose/preference.
- **H-33 An inventory opener closes by reword to a sentence per job.** An opening or scope sentence that lists the page's parts as nominalised noun phrases, after a colon or in a run of commas (`language.md`, "Headings, lists and tables"), takes `W` to the sentence-per-job form the record carries, each sentence with an actor and a verb, when the candidate is eligible under H-29. The cold editor's scope-sentence retain does not apply to this shape, and a retain that cites it is set aside. When no record carries a candidate, the grade is `T` with the instruction "one sentence per job of the page, each with a verb", never `I`. Governs fix/verified and propose/fallback on kinds compression and flow; author on an opening.

## Per-category starting points

The grader starts from these and departs only with a heuristic it can name.

| Tool category | Starting move |
| --- | --- |
| fix/verified, kind fact, citation or reference | `C` when the fact is settled by the set itself (H-10); `R` or `I` when the fact rests on a source outside the set (H-14) |
| any category, kind contradiction | `K` with the side that has the home as the default when both sides are whole statements; `R` or `W` when the contested text is a clause inside a surviving sentence (H-12, H-19); `I` when the other statement is an external system's behaviour, not the set's own (H-14) |
| fix/verified, kind compression, flow, term, residue | `W` when the rewrite keeps every claim and any growth is one of the brief's two cases with `loss` saying what the addition makes clear, else `I` (H-11); subject to H-29 |
| fix/layout | `W` when the grader confirms the benefit of the split or list (H-25, H-26), else `I` |
| propose/unverified, propose/stale | `I` naming the state, except any eligible selected candidate, `R`, `C` or `W` (H-29): the cluster's state is the worst across its members, and a sibling's pending verdict is no prohibition on a cleared candidate the contract selects |
| propose/claim-shift | `R` under H-03 or H-06 when the removal candidate's own verdict permits, else `I` with the verifier's note quoted (H-29) |
| propose/introduced | `I` for the rewrite; an independently verified removal may take `R` (H-29) |
| author/presentation | `O` with the inventory (H-30) |
| propose/grows | `C` when the passage's own promise is unmet and the set supplies the missing part (H-15); `R` of the suspect claim under H-03; else `I` |
| propose/removal, propose/tangent with a span | `R` when the span is a whole sentence, bullet, row or paragraph; otherwise `W` or `C` rewording the containing sentence (H-19). Never a condensation of the passage (H-23) |
| propose/removal, kind flow | `R` on the named sentence when a restatement of the paragraph drops that sentence, else `I` (H-24) |
| discard/tangent | `I` |
| discard/cites-not-found | the cited page does not hold the quoted text, so the basis is unproven and the finding cannot rest on the set. `I`, unless the defect stands without the cite, in which case grade the finding on its own kind and span |
| author/author | `R` when the text's allusion to an undecided point is the defect, else `I` (H-05, H-08); `R` under H-01 or H-02 when the text asserts what is undecided |
| propose/dispersion | the shorter of the alternatives, as `R` or `W` |
| propose/inference | `C` for a one-word correction under H-07 or a completion under H-15, else `I` |
| propose/no-rewrite, propose/restructure | `I`, or `O` under H-08 |
| propose/preference | `W` when the record carries a rewrite citing a named rule, the rewrite keeps every claim, and any growth is one of the brief's two cases with `loss` saying what the addition makes clear; otherwise `I` (H-11); subject to H-29 |
| propose/unresolved | `R` when the item is review residue (H-16), else `I` |

A repeated pass over a page stops when a pass finds no new difficulty the rubric supports. A change that alters no reader outcome, such as a synonym, a reordering the reader does not need, or a second split of a paragraph already on one topic, is churn and takes `I`. Apply not changing a page twice is a property of the tool; stopping is a judgment of the grader.

## The grader's order of work

Diagnose the reader's problem first; remove the unit when its point is dispensable; otherwise reword the unit that must survive. A layout move, a split or a list, is a candidate reword judged by the reader's difficulty, not a preferred one. An explicit rewrite request from the user invokes explain and rewrite and is not replaced by deletion-only findings; explain and rewrite still requires preservation and a supported improvement, so when those fail the source is retained or the unresolved question is named. The grader evaluates the candidates the records carry and never composes a rewrite.

Two rules stand before every step and are named again at the steps that return a move: a cluster whose kinds hold `presentation`, or any member of which is a presentation record, is `O` and takes no other move (H-30); and no `R`, `C` or `W` is returned until H-29's check on the edit record's own candidate and verdict passes, wherever in the steps the diagnosis was made.

1. Is the finding about the text's coherence with itself, or about its correctness against the world (H-14)? External correctness is out of scope: remove unneeded detail or ignore, never correct.
2. State the passage's point. If the point is a judgement (H-01), the move is `R`. If the finding is the review's own residue (H-16), `R`. Subject to H-29.
3. Is the finding describing or demanding a mechanism the design has not decided (H-02)? Then `R` the detail. Subject to H-29.
4. Is the finding a contradiction between the set's own statements (H-12)? When both sides are whole statements, `K` with the side that has the home as the default. When the contested text is a clause inside a sentence that survives, treat the finding as any other defect and carry on to the removal and reword steps.
5. Does the passage promise something its own text does not meet, and does the set supply the missing part (H-15)? Then `C`, growth allowed. Subject to H-29.
6. Prefer removal of a dispensable unit over rewriting it: can the claim go rather than be fixed (H-03, H-04, H-06, H-18, H-21)? Removal means the whole sentence, bullet, row or paragraph. Check the unit the span sits in: when the sentence must survive, the move is a reword of that sentence, never a cut (H-19). Under `clarify`, a split or a list that closes a confirmed reader difficulty is a candidate reword (H-25, H-26), and a framing sentence or an unclear reference closes under H-27 or H-28. Subject to H-29.
7. `C` only for a fact the set itself settles (H-10, H-07, H-22) or a reword that states such a fact. `W` for a cited rule whose rewrite the record carries, keeps every claim and grows only in the brief's two cases (H-11), and for any sentence that has to survive with its defect gone; a navigational pointer with no real target takes `W` to a real heading (H-04). Subject to H-29.
8. A question about who decides, or about what the design should be, is not an editorial finding (H-08): remove the allusion when the allusion is the defect, else ignore.
9. `I` when nothing changes for the reader, when a rule has its home elsewhere (H-05), or when another card closes this one (H-09).
10. Never `T`.
