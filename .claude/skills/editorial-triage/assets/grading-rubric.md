# Grading rubric for editorial triage

The rubric is the shared definition of how a review finding is closed. Every finding gets exactly one closing move; the `GRADER` worker predicts the move, `grade-triage.sh` measures how often the prediction matches the user's move, and the partition rules implement the rubric. The frame is closing, not opening: a finding is resolved by a small change to the text or by recording an open point with an owner, never by a question that invites more text. Change the rubric before changing a partition rule.

A heuristic states a rule of editing and the categories the rule governs. It carries no record of the run, the round or the person that produced the rule. That provenance lives in the calibration files of the run workarea, which is where a later run measures its own agreement. A rule that holds only for one document set is a run note for that review, never a heuristic here.

## The closing moves

| Key | Move | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `R` | Remove | Delete a whole unit: a sentence, a bullet, a table row, a paragraph. The passage's point stands without the removed unit. `R` never cuts a fragment out of a sentence that survives. |
| `C` | Correct | Apply the specific correction the set or a published source establishes: a citation section, a count, a name, a definite article that asserts an undecided choice, the side of a contradiction that has a home. Also the move when the defect sits inside a sentence that must survive and the replacement states a fact the set settles. |
| `W` | Reword | Restate the sentence under a named editorial rule so that the defective part is gone, keeping the meaning and without growth. This is the move whenever the defect sits inside a sentence the passage needs. |
| `K` | Keep one side | A contradiction the set does not settle: one statement survives, the other goes. The user picks; the tool proposes a default. |
| `O` | Record as open | The passage's point depends on a fact the set does not hold. The point is recorded in one line with an owner, for the user to take to that owner; the passage is left as a plain statement of what is undecided. Nothing is asked in chat. |
| `I` | Ignore for now | Nothing changes. The default for style, for omissions whose rule has a home elsewhere, and for cards closed by another card. |
| `T` | Tell me what to do | The user states the closing instruction in their words. The grader never predicts `T`; an instruction that generalises becomes a heuristic. |

A run is judged by how many findings close with `R`, `C` or `W` and by how few `K`, `O` and `T` are left for the user. Growth is a cost: a move that adds words needs a heuristic that allows the growth.

## Heuristics

Each entry: id, statement, and the categories the heuristic governs.

- **H-01 Judgements are removed, not qualified.** A judgement of ease, cost, safety or simplicity standing in for a fact ("a rotation is cheap and signs nobody out", "easy", "simple", "safe") is cut. The fact, if one is needed, is stated without the judgement. Governs every category.
- **H-02 Undecided mechanisms are not described.** Where the design has not decided a mechanism (the delegation grant source, the key rotation procedure, provider selection), the text does not describe one. Cut the detail and state only that the design must handle the consideration. Governs propose/grows, author, fix/verified on contradiction.
- **H-03 A missing explanation is first a suspect claim.** When a finding says a passage fails to explain or name something, ask whether the passage should make the claim at all. A second justification for a decision the paragraph already justifies is removed; a reassurance that a later caveat withdraws is removed. Governs propose/grows, propose/dispersion, propose/tangent.
- **H-04 Pointers are removed unless the reader cannot find the target without them.** Cross-page and same-page pointers atrophy: their targets move, get renamed or vanish, and every one is a dependency to maintain. A pointer whose only job is to announce where something is listed ("listed under Open questions below") goes; a pointer that loops or names a target that does not exist goes. A pointer is kept and fixed (`W` to a real heading) only when the reader would otherwise be unable to find the reason behind a rule ("see the session lifetimes below"). A one-line fact may be restated in place of a pointer. Governs fix/verified, propose/removal, propose/restructure, propose/tangent.
- **H-05 Rules are not restated at their instances.** A rule with a home elsewhere in the set is not repeated where an instance occurs, and the omission is not a defect. Governs discard/tangent, propose/grows, author.
- **H-06 A claim the set contradicts is cut, not hedged.** When another statement in the set says a stated fact is unverified, drop the clause rather than qualify the clause. Governs propose/removal, fix/verified on contradiction.
- **H-07 An undecided choice is not asserted.** Wording that asserts a decided option ("the corporate Entra tenant") is corrected to the indefinite form in one word; no growth. Governs propose/inference, propose/grows.
- **H-08 A design or ownership question is not an editorial finding.** Who decides a design point, what the design should be, and which team answers a question are outside what editorial review owns, which is the text's coherence with itself. Where the text alludes to such an undecided point and the allusion is the defect, remove the allusion (`R`); otherwise ignore the finding (`I`). `O` records a point only where the user asks for one, and then as a single line with an owner, never as a question in chat and never as new text in the page. Governs author, propose/restructure.
- **H-09 One move can close other cards.** A move on one card can resolve other findings on the same passage; the tool records which cards are closed by which, and the closed cards take `I`.
- **H-10 Verified corrections are applied.** A wrong citation section, count, name or actor corrected against a named published source or against the page that holds the fact takes `C`, provided the span occurs once in the page and the rewrite passes the gate. Governs fix/verified.
- **H-11 Rule-derived rewrites apply only without growth, and only when the record carries them.** A rewrite whose basis is a named `language.md` or `document-types.md` section takes `W` when the section covers the text and the rewrite does not grow; a growing rewrite takes `I`, or `R` under H-03. The grader grades the rewrite the record carries; it never composes a new one, so an item with no rewrite on the record cannot take `W` or `C`. Governs fix/verified on compression, flow, term, residue; propose/preference.
- **H-12 A contradiction closes as a choice of side only when both sides are whole statements.** When each side is a statement claiming the design, the move is `K`: one survives and the other goes, with the side that has the home as the default. The home is the structural home of the fact, a mechanism against a bare assertion, or the majority of three or more statements; a preview, summary or restatement of the design is the side that goes. When the contested text is instead a clause inside a sentence that stands without the clause, and the other side is not itself in question, the contradiction is not a choice: the clause goes under `R` if its unit can go, else the sentence is reworded without the clause under H-19. Putting such an item to the user as a choice of side asks them to pick between a sentence and a fragment. Governs every category on kind contradiction.
- **H-13 A guess is never applied.** A proposal that supplies a mechanism, reason or fact the pages do not state, even by one inference, never takes `C` or `W`; under H-02 the guessed detail is cut. An inference with one cited step from the set is not a guess (H-15). Governs propose/inference, author.
- **H-14 Correctness against the world is out of scope.** Editorial review and its triage own the text's coherence with itself, not its accuracy against sources outside the set: an RFC section number, a vendor's documented behaviour, a platform fact. Such claims are fact-checked by a separate process. A finding that rests on external correctness is not corrected: detail the passage does not need is removed (`R`); otherwise the finding is ignored (`I`). Only a contradiction between the set's own statements counts as a contradiction. Governs fix/verified on fact and citation, propose/removal, author.
- **H-15 An incomplete promise is completed.** When a passage states its own purpose (what a prefix guarantees; the cases a walkthrough step covers; the environments a defence covers) and its text does not meet that purpose, and the set supplies the missing part (a stated condition, the other case, a fact the same page states elsewhere), the completion takes `C` and the growth is allowed. The test is the passage's own promise, not a reader's curiosity; H-03 still governs additions the passage never promised. A defence or rule stated for one environment when the page itself places the service in another is an incomplete promise. Governs propose/grows, propose/inference, propose/omission.
- **H-16 Review residue is removed.** A finding that is the review's own meta-line (a legend, a gate statement, a note about the report) takes `R`: the finding is dropped, not ignored, so it never reappears. Governs propose/unresolved, discard.
- **H-17 Equivalent moves count as agreement.** When a correction's text equals the removal's result, the user and the grader choosing differently between the two counts as agreement. The card still offers both, because after H-19 they are different edits: the removal deletes a whole unit, the correction restates a sentence that survives.
- **H-18 The document's own tallies are removed, not corrected.** A count the passage offers about its own bookkeeping (how many registration families, how many rows, how many secret updates, how many decisions) is cut rather than fixed: the reader needs the things, not the number of them, and the number is what goes wrong when the things change. Governs fix/verified on fact, propose/grows, author.
- **H-19 Remove the whole unit, or reword the sentence; never cut a fragment out of a surviving sentence.** A defect inside a sentence the passage needs is closed by rewording that sentence so the defective part is gone, not by deleting the span and leaving whatever remains. `R` applies when the whole sentence, bullet, row or paragraph goes, and also when the span is a complete parenthetical aside, because deleting one leaves the sentence around the aside whole. A pointer in brackets, such as `(see Cookie design, in Cookies and token transport)`, is the common case. Cutting a span mid-sentence produces a bare noun, a stranded subject, a lowercase opening or a line that fails the style gate, and the reviewer's own rewrite is usually already on the record. So ask first whether the whole unit can go, and reword when it cannot. Governs every category.
- **H-20 Retired.** The numbering skips this id and no later rule reuses the number. The rule that held the id applied to one document set rather than to editing technical documentation, so the rule left the rubric. Grades from earlier runs still cite the id, and the tooling reports such a citation as suspect.
- **H-21 A sentence that narrates the document rather than the design is redundant.** Text reporting what the document does, does not do, or has already settled ("that detail is not repeated here", "the IDM facts are established, but the migration approach needs product decisions", "as context for that follow-up") tells the reader what the document itself already shows. The whole sentence goes under `R`, or the containing sentence is reworded without the clause. This is a repetition defect, not an omission. Governs propose/tangent, propose/removal, fix/verified on compression and residue.
- **H-22 A claim about what the document provides is corrected when more is known.** Where the text states what data, list or section the document offers, and the set holds material the statement does not account for, the move is `C` on the statement. The claim is wrong, so correct the claim; do not add material to satisfy the claim, and do not ask. This is an omission defect and is distinct from H-21. Governs author, propose/grows, propose/restructure.

## Per-category starting points

The grader starts from these and departs only with a heuristic it can name.

| Tool category | Starting move |
| --- | --- |
| fix/verified, kind fact, citation or reference | `C` when the fact is settled by the set itself (H-10); `R` or `I` when the fact rests on a source outside the set (H-14) |
| any category, kind contradiction | `K` with the side that has the home as the default when both sides are whole statements; `R` or `W` when the contested text is a clause inside a surviving sentence (H-12, H-19); `I` when the other statement is an external system's behaviour, not the set's own (H-14) |
| fix/verified, kind compression, flow, term, residue | `W` if the rewrite does not grow, else `I` (H-11) |
| propose/grows | `C` when the passage's own promise is unmet and the set supplies the missing part (H-15); `R` of the suspect claim under H-03; else `I` |
| propose/removal, propose/tangent with a span | `R` when the span is a whole sentence, bullet, row or paragraph; otherwise `W` or `C` rewording the containing sentence (H-19) |
| discard/tangent | `I` |
| author/author | `R` when the text's allusion to an undecided point is the defect, else `I` (H-05, H-08); `R` under H-01 or H-02 when the text asserts what is undecided |
| propose/dispersion | the shorter of the alternatives, as `R` or `W` |
| propose/inference | `C` for a one-word correction under H-07 or a completion under H-15, else `I` |
| propose/no-rewrite, propose/restructure | `I`, or `O` under H-08 |
| propose/preference | `W` when the record carries a rewrite citing a named rule and the rewrite does not grow the text; otherwise `I` (H-11) |
| propose/unresolved | `R` when the item is review residue (H-16), else `I` |

## The grader's order of work

1. Is the finding about the text's coherence with itself, or about its correctness against the world (H-14)? External correctness is out of scope: remove unneeded detail or ignore, never correct.
2. State the passage's point. If the point is a judgement (H-01), the move is `R`. If the finding is the review's own residue (H-16), `R`.
3. Is the finding describing or demanding a mechanism the design has not decided (H-02)? Then `R` the detail.
4. Is the finding a contradiction between the set's own statements (H-12)? When both sides are whole statements, `K` with the side that has the home as the default. When the contested text is a clause inside a sentence that survives, treat the finding as any other defect and carry on to the removal and reword steps.
5. Does the passage promise something its own text does not meet, and does the set supply the missing part (H-15)? Then `C`, growth allowed.
6. Prefer removal over any rewrite: can the claim go rather than be fixed (H-03, H-04, H-06, H-18, H-21)? Removal means the whole sentence, bullet, row or paragraph. Check the unit the span sits in: when the sentence must survive, the move is a reword of that sentence, never a cut (H-19).
7. `C` only for a fact the set itself settles (H-10, H-07, H-22) or a reword that states such a fact. `W` for a cited rule with no growth and a rewrite the record carries (H-11), and for any sentence that has to survive with its defect gone; a navigational pointer with no real target takes `W` to a real heading (H-04).
8. A question about who decides, or about what the design should be, is not an editorial finding (H-08): remove the allusion when the allusion is the defect, else ignore.
9. `I` when nothing changes for the reader, when a rule has its home elsewhere (H-05), or when another card closes this one (H-09).
10. Never `T`.
